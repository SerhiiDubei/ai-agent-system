# Working Page Patterns — Senior Care

Industry: walk-in tubs, mobility aids, hearing aids, Medicare advisory, senior living, fall-detection wearables, hospice, in-home care.

## What working senior-care LPs look like

### Structural signs (high-load preservation candidates)

- **Phone number prominently above the fold** — often THE single biggest trust signal for 65+ audience. Visible click-to-call on mobile.
- **BBB A+ badge** above-the-fold or near the form
- **State licensing / certification** numbers visible (varies by service: contractor's license for installers, NAIC for insurance, etc.)
- **"Since [year]" — 10+ years** prominently shown, with year specific
- **Specific number of customers served + geography**: "4,500+ Florida seniors served" beats "thousands"
- **Real installer / staff photos with first names**, not stock
- **Testimonial wall with senior women** (audience self-selection signal)
- **"Free in-home assessment" / "no obligation"** language explicit
- **"No high-pressure sales"** language explicit (overcomes a known objection)
- **Veteran discount / military discount** visible if applicable

### Form patterns

- **3-4 fields typical**: ZIP + name + phone + (optional email)
- **Phone field IS THE primary contact**, not email — senior audience prefers phone follow-up
- **"Submit"/"Get Started" rarely**; specific value-prop submit copy preferred ("Get My Free Assessment", "See if We Serve Your Area")
- **Privacy reassurance near form** — "We don't share your info"

### Copy patterns

- **Hero headline is fear-of-loss-anchored** OR **identity-preservation-anchored**:
  - Fear-of-loss: "Stop fearing a fall in the shower" / "Falls put 1 in 4 seniors in the hospital"
  - Identity: "Stay safe and independent at home" / "Aging-in-place, with peace of mind"
- **Subhead clarifies offer**: "Free in-home assessment from a local installer"
- **Body emphasizes independence + family + dignity**
- **Decision-helper language present**: "If you're caring for a parent..."

### Visual patterns

- **Photos of seniors in dignified, capable poses** (not "frail" stock photos)
- **Multi-generational imagery** acknowledges decision-helper
- **Floor-plan / before-after for installation niches**
- **Larger fonts than other industries** (16px+ body, 24px+ headlines on mobile)
- **High contrast** — minimum 7:1 for body copy

## Preservation defaults (DO NOT TOUCH without extraordinary evidence)

For a senior-care page hitting these patterns:

- **Phone number above fold** — load: ~15-20% on cold Meta
- **BBB / state license** — load: ~15-25%
- **Specific number + geography** ("4,500 Florida seniors") — load: ~10-15%
- **"No high-pressure sales" language** — load: ~5-10% (overcomes the senior-services-call-center objection)
- **Real staff photos with names** — load: ~5-10%
- **Years-in-business "Since 19XX"** — load: ~5-10%
- **Decision-helper acknowledgment in copy** — load: ~5-10%

## Change-safe zones (typical for working senior-care LPs)

- Below-fold benefit list ordering
- Hero subheadline (test variants but anchor to working tone)
- Specific imagery (not portrait orientation/composition)
- Footer trust signals (less load than above-fold)
- FAQ section content
- "How it works" step descriptions

## Anti-patterns I've seen destroy senior-care conversion

- **"Modernizing" the page** by removing the BBB badge → conversion -8% to -15%
- **Replacing real installer photos with stock** → -5% to -10%
- **Removing the phone number** for "cleaner mobile" → -10%+ on senior cold traffic
- **Adding pop-ups / modal urgency** ("Call now! Limited spots!") → -5% AND brand damage
- **Reducing form to just ZIP** for "frictionless" → +clicks but -lead quality (wrong-fit leads)
- **Removing the "no high-pressure sales" text** because "negative framing" → -5% to -8%

## When prior_tests_tried mentions form changes

If operator has already tested "reduced form 4→3 fields" in this niche:
- The obvious form-pruning win is harvested
- Future form tests should test field ORDER, not field count
- Or test multi-step (1 field per screen) vs single-page
- Or test phone-required vs phone-optional

## Industries that follow this pattern

- Walk-in tubs (Kohler walk-in bath, Safe Step, American Standard walk-in)
- Hearing aids (Eargo, Lively, Audicus)
- Stairlifts (Acorn, Bruno)
- Mobility aids (Pride Scooter, Drive Medical)
- Senior living (A Place for Mom, Caring.com)
- In-home care (Visiting Angels, Home Instead)
- Medicare advisory (eHealth, GoHealth)
- Fall detection (Lively, Medical Guardian)

## Industries that DEVIATE from this pattern

- Insurance for seniors (less form-heavy, more comparison-quiz pattern)
- Senior tech products (cell phones for seniors — more ecom-like, less trust-heavy)
- Subscription pill organizers — closer to DTC ecom pattern
