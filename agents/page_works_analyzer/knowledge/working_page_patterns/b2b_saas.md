# Working Page Patterns — B2B SaaS

Industry: workflow tools, CRM, dev tools, analytics, security, HR tech, AI infra. Multi-stakeholder buying, longer sales cycles, security-sensitive.

## What working B2B SaaS LPs look like

### Structural signs (high-load preservation candidates)

- **SOC 2 Type II badge** prominently displayed (table stakes >$25k ACV)
- **ISO 27001** for international/enterprise
- **HIPAA / GDPR / CCPA** badges where relevant
- **Named customer logos** in the ICP — not random Fortune-500, but companies the visitor recognizes as PEERS
- **G2 Leader badge** in the right quadrant (Spring/Summer/Fall/Winter)
- **Capterra / TrustRadius** ratings
- **Specific named integrations** (Salesforce, Slack, GitHub, etc.)
- **"Used by [N] companies"** with specific large number
- **Status page link** (uptime credibility)
- **DPA / Trust Center** linked in footer

### Form patterns

- **"Book a demo"** for sales-led products — usually 4-6 fields (name, email, company, role, company size, sometimes phone)
- **"Start free trial"** for PLG products — 1-2 fields (email + sometimes name) OR Google/Microsoft SSO
- **Calendly embed for sales** — often outperforms form on demo flows

### Copy patterns

- **Hero headline names a SPECIFIC pain or category-replacement**:
  - "[Old tool] but for [new context]"
  - "Stop [specific frustration]"
  - "The [category] for teams that [specific characteristic]"
- **Subhead clarifies the WHO**: "Used by engineering leaders at..."
- **Specific metric anchors**: "Ship 2x faster" (with case-study citation) > "Be more productive"
- **ICP-explicit copy**: speaks to one tribe (e.g. "for Series B engineering orgs"), not "every business"

### Visual patterns

- **Product screenshot above the fold** — often biggest single-element conversion driver
- **Animated GIF or video** of the product in action (not just static)
- **Logo wall of recognized customers** — first thing after hero
- **Case-study quotes with first name + role + company logo**

## Preservation defaults (DO NOT TOUCH without extraordinary evidence)

For a B2B SaaS LP hitting these patterns:

- **Customer logo wall (in-ICP)** — load: ~15-25%
- **Hero product screenshot** — load: ~15-25%
- **SOC 2 / security badges** — load: ~10-15% on infosec-sensitive products
- **G2 Leader badge** — load: ~5-10%
- **Specific case-study with metric** — load: ~10-15%
- **Named integration logos** — load: ~5-10%
- **DPA / Trust Center link** — load: ~3-5% (small but critical for procurement)

## Change-safe zones

- Below-fold feature explainer copy
- FAQ section
- Footer copy
- Pricing-page secondary CTA copy
- Testimonial REORDERING (not removal of recognized brands)

## Anti-patterns I've seen destroy B2B SaaS conversion

- **Removing SOC 2 / security badges** for "design clarity" → -8% to -15% on enterprise leads
- **Replacing specific case-study quote with generic "join thousands"** → -10%
- **Removing customer logo wall** for "modern minimal" → -15% to -20%
- **Replacing "Book a demo" with "Get started"** on sales-led product → +clicks but -qualified-leads
- **Removing pricing entirely** ("contact sales") on PLG product → -25%+ trial signups

## When prior_tests_tried mentions hero changes

If operator has tested hero-headline variants already, the WORKING headline is a strong signal of message-market fit. Future tests should:
- Test SUBHEAD variants (lower-risk than re-testing hero)
- Test product-screenshot variants (different feature highlighted)
- Test CTA copy variants
- Avoid further hero-headline tests unless something fundamental changed (new ICP, new pricing tier)

## Industries that follow this pattern

- Workflow tools (Linear, Asana, Notion enterprise, ClickUp)
- CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive)
- Dev tools (Vercel, Sentry, LaunchDarkly)
- Analytics (Mixpanel, Amplitude, PostHog)
- Security (Vanta, Drata, Snyk)
- HR tech (Rippling, Gusto, BambooHR)

## Industries that DEVIATE from this pattern

- B2B services (consulting, agencies) — closer to lead-capture pattern
- B2B vertical SaaS for blue-collar (construction, restaurants) — heavier trust signals + lower digital literacy
- Enterprise procurement-only (>$500k ACV) — usually no LP at all; gated by sales
