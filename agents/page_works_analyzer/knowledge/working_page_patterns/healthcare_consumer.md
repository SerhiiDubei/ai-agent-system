# Working Page Patterns — Healthcare Consumer

Industry: telehealth, mental health apps, health tracking, prescription delivery, fertility, sleep, mental wellness apps.

## What working healthcare-consumer LPs look like

### Structural signs (high-load preservation candidates)

- **HIPAA compliance** explicit (called out, not assumed)
- **Licensed clinicians** state-by-state visibility for telehealth
- **"Insurance accepted"** with logos (BCBS, Aetna, Cigna, etc.)
- **"No surprise billing"** explicit
- **Named medical director** with credentials
- **Board certifications** visible
- **App Store / Play Store rating** for apps
- **Privacy policy** linked + "your data is never sold" callout
- **Cancellation policy** visible — huge in mental health where users feel trapped

### Form patterns

- **Short intake flow** (3-5 questions to assess need)
- **"Take the assessment"** common pattern (quiz as triage)
- **Insurance check** as early step (filters cash-pay shock)
- **"Match to a therapist in 24 hours"** specific timeline

### Copy patterns

- **Hero headline destigmatizes**:
  - "Talk to someone today" (not "treat your depression")
  - "Therapy on your schedule" (not "manage your mental illness")
  - Identity-protective framing per Kahan
- **Subhead names the audience**: "For new moms", "For couples", "For anxiety"
- **Body emphasizes confidentiality + accessibility**
- **Cost transparency**: "$X/month" or "Insurance covers most plans"

### Visual patterns

- **Real photos of relatable people** (not stock "depressed" people)
- **Clinician photos with credentials**
- **Mobile-first layout** — most healthcare conversion happens on mobile
- **Calming color palettes** (not clinical/sterile)

## Preservation defaults (DO NOT TOUCH without extraordinary evidence)

For a healthcare-consumer LP hitting these patterns:

- **HIPAA badge / compliance language** — load: ~15-20% (the privacy-paranoid audience checks)
- **Insurance logos** — load: ~15-20% on telehealth (kills cash-pay shock)
- **"No surprise billing" language** — load: ~5-10%
- **Licensed clinician count + state coverage** — load: ~10-15%
- **App store ratings** — load: ~5-10% for apps
- **Cancellation policy clarity** — load: ~5-10% on mental health
- **Identity-protective framing** in hero — load: ~10-15%
- **Insurance-check flow** — load: ~10-15% (filters before commitment)

## Change-safe zones

- Below-fold feature/benefit copy
- "How it works" step descriptions
- FAQ content
- Footer trust/legal links
- Image variants of relatable people (not the medical-director photo)

## Anti-patterns I've seen destroy healthcare-consumer conversion

- **Removing HIPAA callout** for "design simplicity" → -10% to -15%
- **Replacing real-person photos with abstract illustrations** → -8% to -12%
- **Removing insurance logos** in favor of "we accept all insurance" → -15%+ (audience needs to see THEIR insurance)
- **Aggressive "Get Help Now"** CTAs on mental-health → trust erosion + bounce
- **Hiding cancellation policy** for "less friction" → +signups but huge LTV/refund problems
- **Pathologizing language** ("treat your depression" vs "talk to a therapist") → triggers identity rejection

## When prior_tests_tried mentions intake-flow changes

If operator has tested intake length/sequence:
- Intake design is validated as load-bearing
- Future tests should test question ORDER, copy, OR add insurance-check earlier
- NOT total intake removal

## Industries that follow this pattern

- Telehealth therapy (Talkspace, BetterHelp, Cerebral, Brightline)
- General telehealth (Sesame Care, MDLive, Teladoc)
- Health tracking (Whoop, Oura)
- Fertility (Cofertility, Modern Fertility)
- Prescription delivery (Hims, Ro, Hims/Hers)
- Mental wellness apps (Calm, Headspace, Wysa)

## Industries that DEVIATE from this pattern

- Direct primary care ($X/mo memberships) — heavier on relationship/trust, less on insurance
- Concierge medicine — closer to B2B premium pattern
- Disease-specific platforms (oncology, cardiac) — even heavier compliance emphasis, longer sales cycles
- Wellness coaching (non-clinical) — closer to DTC subscription pattern (less HIPAA-heavy)
