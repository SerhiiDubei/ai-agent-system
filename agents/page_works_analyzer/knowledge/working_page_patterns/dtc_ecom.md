# Working Page Patterns — DTC Ecommerce

Industry: subscription wellness, supplements, beauty, skincare, fitness, food/meal kits.

## What working DTC LPs look like

### Structural signs (high-load preservation candidates)

- **Real customer photos with first names + cities** (UGC > studio)
- **Star rating + review count** above the fold (specific count, not "thousands")
- **Third-party testing badges** (NSF, USP, Informed Choice for supplements; cGMP)
- **Founder story OR MD on advisory board** prominently linked
- **Money-back guarantee** with specific terms (30-day, 90-day, etc.)
- **Trustpilot / Yotpo aggregate** display
- **Subscription-friendly cancellation copy** ("Skip or cancel anytime — no questions")
- **As seen in / press logos** (esp. for newer brands)
- **"Made in [USA / specific country]"** where applicable

### Form/checkout patterns

- **Quiz-as-trojan-horse** common pattern (lowers commitment, personalizes output, raises perceived fit)
- **Single-field email signup** for "newsletter discount" (10-15% off first order)
- **Subscription checkbox pre-checked** with monthly cancel reminder
- **Mobile sticky add-to-cart** essential

### Copy patterns

- **Hero headline operational, not aspirational**:
  - "Ditch the shelf full of half-used vitamins"
  - "The greens powder for people who've tried 5 already"
  - "Skin care that survives travel"
- **Subhead names the segment**: "For 30-something women juggling..."
- **Body uses "I" voice** in customer testimonials (vs branded copy)
- **"Why we exist" founder story** if recently founded (within 5 years)
- **NO cliches**: avoid "revolutionary", "premium", "world-class" — they tank credibility on this audience

### Visual patterns

- **Lifestyle photography of REAL customers** (not models)
- **Founder's actual face** if founder-led brand
- **Product photography that shows scale + use context** (not just clean product shots)
- **Before/after photos with disclosures** where category allows
- **Ingredient transparency visuals**

## Preservation defaults (DO NOT TOUCH without extraordinary evidence)

For a DTC ecom LP hitting these patterns:

- **Star rating + review count** above fold — load: ~15-20%
- **Real customer photos with names** — load: ~10-15%
- **Founder story** if present — load: ~10-15%
- **Money-back guarantee** — load: ~5-10%
- **Third-party testing badges** for supplements — load: ~10-15% (the audience checks)
- **Subscription cancellation reassurance** — load: ~5-10% (overcomes a known objection)
- **Quiz funnel** if present — load: ~15-25% (the entire conversion mechanism)
- **Press / "as seen in" logos** — load: ~5-10%

## Change-safe zones

- Below-fold benefit list ordering
- Quiz question copy variants (NOT question count)
- Lifestyle photo variants
- Color palette within brand guidelines
- Footer testimonial selection

## Anti-patterns I've seen destroy DTC conversion

- **Replacing real customer photos with studio models** → -10% to -15%
- **Removing review count for "cleaner" star display** → -8% to -12%
- **Removing founder story for "scale" feel** on early-stage brand → -10%+
- **Hiding subscription cancellation** for "less friction" → +signups but -LTV (and brand damage)
- **Adding aggressive scarcity** ("only 5 left!") on a category that doesn't support it → trust erosion
- **Overusing "premium"/"luxury"** language to a $60-100k HHI audience → alienation
- **Removing "made in USA"** for global brand feel → varies (audience-dependent)

## When prior_tests_tried mentions quiz

If operator has tested quiz variations:
- Quiz is load-bearing
- Future tests should test question SEQUENCE, output personalization copy, or post-quiz checkout flow
- NOT quiz removal — that's a category-level paradigm shift, not an A/B test

## Industries that follow this pattern

- Subscription supplements (AG1, Ritual, Care/of)
- Beauty subscription (Birchbox, Curology)
- Health DTC (Hims, Hers, Ro)
- Fitness equipment (Peloton, Tonal)
- Meal kits (Hello Fresh, Blue Apron)
- Skincare DTC (Glossier, The Ordinary)

## Industries that DEVIATE from this pattern

- Luxury fashion DTC ($300+ AOV) — heavier on aspirational imagery, less on UGC
- B2C2B (sold to consumers but procured through HR) — closer to B2B SaaS pattern
- One-off ecom (no subscription, $50-150 AOV) — closer to traditional ecom pattern
