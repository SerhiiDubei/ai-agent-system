# Market Segment: Senior Care

Covers: walk-in tubs, mobility aids, hearing aids, Medicare advisory, senior living, in-home care, hospice, fall-detection wearables, stairlifts, aging-in-place modifications.

## Typical primary buyer profile
**The adult daughter**, age 48-62, household income $75-150k, high digital literacy. **NOT the elder.** The elder is the user; the daughter is the buyer, researcher, and credit card holder.

## Decision-maker structure
**Triangulated.** Daughter researches and recommends; elder must consent (identity/autonomy); often a sibling (especially a brother) blocks on cost or "Mom's fine." Spouse of the daughter is often a silent veto on time/money.

## Common decision-helper / blocker persona
- **Out-of-town sibling** who doesn't see daily decline and resists changes ("you're overreacting")
- **Elder's PCP** — one offhand comment can kill or close a sale
- **Adult son** typically more conservative on cost than adult daughter

## Awareness stage distribution
- ~40% Problem-Aware (something's wrong, no plan)
- ~30% Solution-Aware (researching options)
- ~15% Product-Aware (comparing 2-3 vendors)
- ~10% Unaware (still in denial)
- ~5% Most-Aware

Bias your funnel for problem→solution-aware. Cold-traffic LPs should mostly target Problem-Aware.

## Income realism / "the lie"
Marketers assume seniors have nest eggs. Reality: median 65+ household income ~$50k; ~50% of single elders rely on Social Security for >50% of income. **The daughter is paying.** Price ladders should assume the daughter's budget ($75-150k HHI), not the elder's assets.

For personas: assign elder personas income bands `under_30k` or `30_60k`. Adult-child personas: `60_100k` or `100k_plus`.

## Common pain trigger patterns
Almost never gradual — it's an EVENT:
- A *hospital discharge*
- A *fall*
- A *missed-medication scare*
- A *driving incident*
- A *spouse's death*
- *Snowbird-return shock* ("Mom's much worse than at Christmas")
- Cognitive decline noticed during a holiday gathering (Thanksgiving is the classic)

## Anti-personas to avoid
- The "spry retiree golfing in khakis" stock-photo trope (real audience is exhausted, scared, grieving an earlier version of their parent)
- The "tech-illiterate boomer" trope — 60+ adults are heavy Facebook and YouTube users
- The "wealthy-retiree-with-time" persona — for paid services, the buyer is usually budget-stretched and time-poor

## Channel context
- **Meta (especially Facebook)** — daughter scrolls in waiting rooms
- Google search ("home care near me," "Medicare cover…")
- AARP / Caring.com referral
- NextDoor
- Church bulletins
- Hospital discharge planner referrals

## Trust signals that move the needle
- State licensure number
- BBB A+ rating
- "Bonded & insured caregivers"
- "Background-checked" caregivers
- Medicare/Medicaid acceptance (if applicable)
- Joint Commission accreditation
- AARP partnerships
- **Named care coordinator (a real human)**
- Local owner story (real photo, real name)
- **Photos of REAL caregivers (not stock)**

## Required cardinal-error check
**Any 55+ niche brief MUST have at least one persona with role="decision_helper".** This is non-negotiable. Skipping it produces marketing that talks past the actual buyer.

## Frameworks I always pair with this segment
- `caregiver_burden.md` (Zarit/Pearlin) — for the daughter persona's pains
- `health_belief_model.md` — for fear-driven preventive purchases (fall detection, etc.)
- `identity_protective_cognition.md` — elder identity = "independent"; reframe accordingly
- `maslow_pain_mapping.md` — safety + esteem + belonging tier stack
