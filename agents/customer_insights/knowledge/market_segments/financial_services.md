# Market Segment: Financial Services Consumer

Covers: debt relief, credit repair, lending (personal loans, payday alternatives, BNPL), insurance (life, home, auto), tax services, budgeting/HYSA fintech, investment advisory direct-to-consumer.

## Typical primary buyer profile
- Age 28-55 (debt segment skews lower; insurance skews wider)
- Household income $35-85k (some segments wider)
- Mobile-first
- Often unbanked-adjacent or thinly-banked (for debt/lending)
- Digital literacy moderate to high

## Decision-maker structure
Usually sole — but **spouse approval** is the silent gate for anything $200/mo+. For insurance specifically, joint decision is the norm. **Shame keeps spouses uninformed in debt cases — a hidden conversion barrier.**

## Common decision-helper / blocker persona
- **Spouse** (especially for insurance)
- A "money-savvy" friend or sibling who's been asked to vet the offer
- Critically — the **prior bad actor**: customer has been burned (or knows someone burned) by a sketchy debt-relief or payday outfit. **That ghost is in every conversation.**

## Awareness stage distribution
- Debt relief / credit repair audience: ~50% Problem-Aware, ~30% Solution-Aware (they've tried things)
- Insurance audience: skews Unaware/Problem-Aware (life insurance avoidance is famous)
- Lending varies by category (car loan = Most-Aware; consolidation = Solution-Aware)

## Income realism / "the lie"
Marketers picture a $90k household "consolidating $20k of debt." Real median debt-relief lead: $45k income, $18k unsecured debt, **one income-shock event (medical, divorce, layoff) in the last 18 months.** They are NOT bad with money — they were overwhelmed by a specific event.

For personas: don't default to middle-class. Most personas in this segment land at `30_60k` or `60_100k`.

## Common pain trigger patterns
- Medical bill
- Divorce
- Job loss
- Garnishment notice
- Car repo threat
- A single collections call **at work**
- Tax season shock
- A wedding/baby that exposed the gap

## Anti-personas to avoid
- The "irresponsible spender" trope — offensive and wrong; usually it's a medical or divorce event
- The "financially illiterate" trope — most leads know exactly what APR means
- Wealth-aspirational imagery — alienates this audience

## Channel context
- Google search (high intent: "settle credit card debt," "lower car payment")
- Meta retargeting
- YouTube pre-roll
- Direct mail (still works for 50+)
- Comparison aggregators (Credit Karma, NerdWallet, Bankrate)
- TikTok for under-35s
- Spanish-language radio in select metros

## Trust signals that move the needle
- BBB rating (A+ table stakes)
- AFCC / IAPDA membership for debt relief
- NMLS license number for lending
- A.M. Best rating for insurance
- FDIC/NCUA for deposits
- *Years in business*
- Trustpilot volume (not just score)
- Real client stories with first names + amounts saved
- "No upfront fees" (FTC-mandated for debt relief — call it out anyway)
