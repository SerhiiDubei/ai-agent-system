# Market Segment: B2B SaaS

Covers: workflow tools, CRM, dev tools, analytics, security, HR tech, marketing tech, AI infra.

## Typical primary buyer profile
**Depends on motion.**

- **PLG** (product-led growth): an end-user IC, age 26-40, technical or ops-savvy
- **Sales-led**: a director or VP, 35-50
- **Procurement**: 40+, never the user

Income is irrelevant; **budget authority** is the variable.

## Decision-maker structure
**Almost never sole.** Typical buying committee:
- **Champion** — the IC who wants it
- **Economic Buyer** — signs the PO
- **Technical/Security Buyer** — IT, infosec, must clear it
- **End Users** — will revolt if forced
- **Procurement** — increasingly squeezes terms

For deals >$25k, expect 5-7 stakeholders (Gartner reports ~6.8 average for complex B2B).

## Common decision-helper / blocker persona
- **InfoSec / IT** is the #1 deal-killer at $50k+ ACV
- **Procurement** is #2
- **CFO scrutiny** since 2023 is a third — every tool now needs an *ROI story* the champion can paste into a Slack DM

For B2B briefs, your personas should ALWAYS include at least one champion + one blocker (IT, procurement, or CFO).

## Awareness stage distribution
**Highly skewed by channel:**
- Outbound = mostly Unaware / Problem-Aware
- Inbound (G2 / branded search) = Solution-Aware / Product-Aware
- PLG signups = Most-Aware

Common mistake: writing all marketing for the Most-Aware bucket because that's who shows up at demo.

## Income realism / "the lie"
Marketers assume the champion has authority. They almost never do. **Champion has *influence* and a budget ceiling (often $5-10k/yr they can expense).** Anything above triggers procurement and a 60-90 day cycle. Build the marketing for the champion-to-committee handoff, not the champion alone.

For personas: don't use household income — use `unknown` or skip. Use the persona's organizational role as the meaningful axis.

## Common pain trigger patterns
- A missed quarter
- A board meeting going badly
- A security incident
- A key person leaving (knowledge loss)
- A competitor adopting the tool
- A re-org
- End-of-budget-year "use it or lose it"
- Renewal of an incumbent triggering re-evaluation

## Anti-personas to avoid
- The "lone visionary buyer" trope (committee always exists)
- The "rational ROI calculator" trope (decisions are political and emotional, then justified with ROI)
- Imagining the buyer reads your blog (they skim G2, ask peers, look at your pricing page)
- "Tech-savvy millennial professional" — every B2B persona ever, useless

## Channel context
- Google branded + category search
- G2 / Capterra / Gartner Peer Insights
- Peer Slack/Discord communities
- LinkedIn (esp. for ICP targeting)
- Podcasts
- Conference sponsorships
- Partner ecosystems
- Outbound SDR
- Founder-led X/LinkedIn for early-stage

## Trust signals that move the needle
- **SOC 2 Type II** (table stakes >$25k ACV)
- **ISO 27001** (international/enterprise)
- HIPAA/GDPR/CCPA where relevant
- *Named customer logos in the ICP*
- G2 Leader badge in the right quadrant
- Named integrations (esp. Salesforce, Slack, the customer's stack)
- A clear DPA (Data Processing Agreement)
- Status page uptime
- Named human in support
- "No rip-and-replace" reassurance
