# Market Segment: DTC Ecommerce

Covers: subscription wellness, supplements, beauty, skincare, food/meal kits, fitness equipment, apparel, home goods.

## Typical primary buyer profile
- **Female-skew 65-75%** for wellness/beauty/supplements
- Age 28-55 (subscription wellness skews 30-45; menopause/longevity skews 45-65)
- Household income $60-150k
- Mobile-first
- Instagram/TikTok native
- Comfortable with subscriptions but **increasingly suspicious of them** (post-2023 cancel-culture around DTC subscriptions)

## Decision-maker structure
- Sole for products <$80/mo
- Joint for anything that hits the household credit card visibly or implies lifestyle change (>$150/mo, food delivery, fitness equipment)
- **Spouse-as-mocker is a real friction** ("you bought *what* powder?")

## Common decision-helper / blocker persona
- **The friend group chat** (one screenshot sells more than any ad)
- The skeptical spouse
- **The Reddit r/[category] thread** — buyers Google "[brand] reddit" before checkout
- Increasingly, **Dr. Google** / a creator they trust

## Awareness stage distribution
- Cold Meta = ~50% Problem-Aware, ~30% Solution-Aware, ~10% Unaware, ~10% Product/Most-Aware (warm)
- Branded search = mostly Most-Aware
- Influencer-driven traffic = wildly variable, skews Solution/Product-Aware with high commit-intent

## Income realism / "the lie"
Marketers assume "premium wellness consumer" = $200k+ HHI urban professional. **Reality is a much wider middle: $60-100k HHI suburban women who *prioritize* wellness within a constrained budget.** They'll buy your $48 powder and switch back to Costco for everything else.

Don't price-anchor against luxury; price-anchor against the **cost of not having the outcome**.

## Common pain trigger patterns
- A birthday with a 0 or 5
- A postpartum hormonal shift
- A perimenopause symptom
- A friend's transformation post
- A doctor's offhand "your labs are borderline"
- A breakup or new relationship
- "Summer's coming"
- A creator's "I tried it for 30 days" video

## Anti-personas to avoid
- "Thin white pilates girl" stock trope (alienates 60% of audience that doesn't look like that)
- "Vain consumer" framing (the actual driver is usually energy, sleep, mood — not vanity)
- "Uneducated consumer" — DTC wellness buyers research more than marketers think

## Channel context
- Meta (still #1 by spend for cold acquisition)
- TikTok organic + Spark Ads
- YouTube creators
- Google branded search ("[brand] reviews")
- Amazon (often the *real* checkout even when discovered on DTC)
- Email/SMS for retention
- Podcast host-reads
- Reddit (research, not acquisition)

## Trust signals that move the needle
- Third-party testing (NSF, USP, Informed Choice for supplements; cGMP)
- **Founder story (esp. health-issue origin)**
- *Real customer photos with first names + cities* (UGC > studio)
- MD on advisory board (bonus if she has a TikTok)
- Money-back guarantee with no friction
- Trustpilot/Yotpo volume
- Ingredient transparency
- "Made in [USA / specific country]" where relevant
