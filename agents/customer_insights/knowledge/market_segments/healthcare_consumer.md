# Market Segment: Healthcare Consumer

Covers: telehealth (mental health, primary care, men's/women's health), health tracking apps, medical device DTC, prescription delivery, fertility, sleep, mental wellness apps.

## Typical primary buyer profile
- Age 22-45 for mental health telehealth (skews younger and female)
- Age 30-65 for general telehealth and tracking
- Income $40-120k
- High digital literacy
- **Privacy-sensitive in a way other categories aren't**

## Decision-maker structure
Sole, but with **invisible others**:
- **Employer** (for benefits-mediated)
- **Insurer** (for covered care)
- **Spouse** (for anything that surfaces on EOB statements)
- **Parent** (for under-26s on family plans)

Privacy concern often defeats logical decision.

## Common decision-helper / blocker persona
- **Dr. Google**
- A friend who already uses the service
- The user's existing PCP (whose offhand approval/disapproval can decide)
- Critically — **the EOB**. *"Will this show up on my parents' / spouse's insurance statement?"* is a dealbreaker question that most LPs don't address.

## Awareness stage distribution
- Mental health: ~50% Problem-Aware, ~20% Unaware/in-denial
- Telehealth GP: skews Solution-Aware
- Health tracking: skews Solution/Product-Aware (the buyer self-identifies as a "health-tracker" already)

## Income realism / "the lie"
Marketers picture the "self-pay $300/mo concierge user." **Real median user wants insurance to cover it, will abandon at the first surprise charge, and is choosing between your $99/mo and not getting care at all.** Cash-pay sticker shock is the #1 abandonment driver.

For personas: assume `40_60k` or `60_100k` income, NOT high earners. Assume insurance considerations are top-of-mind in objections.

## Common pain trigger patterns
- A panic attack
- A sleepless week
- A relationship rupture
- A doctor saying "your labs are concerning"
- A missed period
- A parenting crisis
- Post-COVID lingering symptoms
- A workplace incident
- A New Year resolution

## Anti-personas to avoid
- "Wellness-optimizing biohacker" (a tiny minority)
- "Mentally ill = unable to function" trope (most users are functioning and hiding it well)
- "Old people don't do telehealth" (false — 65+ adoption surged post-2020)

## Channel context
- Google search (very high intent)
- Meta (esp. for mental health, with strict ad policy compliance)
- TikTok (under-30 mental health esp.)
- Employer-benefit portals
- Insurer directories
- Reddit (huge research channel for mental health)
- Podcast host-reads

## Trust signals that move the needle
- HIPAA compliance (called out, not assumed)
- Licensed clinicians (state-by-state listed)
- **Insurance accepted (logos)**
- **No surprise billing**
- Named medical director
- Board certifications visible
- BBB
- "Your data is never sold" + a real privacy policy
- App Store/Play Store rating
- A clear cancellation policy (huge in mental health where users feel trapped)

## Frameworks I always pair with this segment
- `health_belief_model.md` — fear-driven, preventive, barrier-heavy
- `identity_protective_cognition.md` — "I'm not the kind of person who needs therapy"
- `caregiver_burden.md` — when the buyer is a parent/spouse caring for someone with chronic illness/mental health
- `maslow_pain_mapping.md` — physiological + safety tiers dominate
