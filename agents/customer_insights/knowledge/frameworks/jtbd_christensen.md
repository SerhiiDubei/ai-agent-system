# Christensen's Jobs-to-be-Done

## Definition
A "Job-to-be-Done" is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance. Customers don't buy products; they "hire" them to do a job. Every job has THREE dimensions: **functional** (the practical task), **emotional** (how they want to feel), and **social** (how they want to be perceived). The unit of analysis is the *job + circumstance*, not demographics.

## Cross-industry application examples

- **DTC supplements**: Functional = "lower my cortisol." Emotional = "feel like I'm finally doing something for myself." Social = "be the friend who has it together at 40, not the one falling apart."
- **B2B SaaS (project tool)**: Functional = "stop status meetings from eating Thursdays." Emotional = "feel in control of my team's week." Social = "look like a competent EM to my director."
- **Senior care (in-home aide)**: Functional = "Dad doesn't fall again." Emotional = "I can sleep through the night without dread." Social = "I'm not the daughter who 'put him in a home.'"
- **Fintech (debt consolidation)**: Functional = "one payment, lower rate." Emotional = "stop feeling sick when I check the mail." Social = "I'm a person who handles their money, not someone who hides bills."
- **Telehealth therapy**: Functional = "talk to a licensed person this week." Emotional = "feel like I'm not crazy." Social = "I can keep this private from my employer/family."

## When I reach for it
**Every persona, no exceptions.** If I have not named all three jobs (functional + emotional + social), the persona is incomplete. The emotional job is usually 3-5× the size of the functional one; the social job is the secret leverage.

## Anti-pattern
Confusing the *job* with the *task*. "Drilling a hole" is a task. "Hanging the family photo before in-laws arrive Saturday" is a job. Also: writing only functional jobs and ignoring emotional/social — that's where almost all marketing leverage actually lives.

## Source
Christensen, *Competing Against Luck* (2016); HBR "Know Your Customers' Jobs to be Done" (Sept 2016).
