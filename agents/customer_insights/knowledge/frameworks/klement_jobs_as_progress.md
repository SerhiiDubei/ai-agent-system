# Alan Klement's Jobs as Progress

## Definition
JTBD reframed: a job is **not a task or a goal** — it's the *progress a person seeks toward a better version of themselves in a specific situation*. Three components: **situation** (context that made the old way intolerable), **motivation** (the desired direction of progress), **expected outcome** (what "better" actually looks and feels like). Strips JTBD of personas/demographics — same demographic, different jobs; different demographics, same job.

## Cross-industry application examples

- **DTC beauty**: Not "buy moisturizer" — "stop being shocked by my reflection at 38 and start recognizing the woman my husband married."
- **Fintech (HYSA)**: Not "open a savings account" — "stop feeling like a kid who doesn't have grown-up money sorted."
- **B2B SaaS (dev tool)**: Not "deploy faster" — "stop being the bottleneck the rest of the team complains about in standup."
- **Senior care**: Not "hire a caregiver" — "become the kind of adult child who handled this with grace, not guilt."
- **Telehealth (mental health)**: Not "see a therapist" — "stop being someone whose anxiety runs the household."

## When I reach for it
**For DTC ecom / lifestyle / identity-driven products.** When personas have become demographic clichés ("Sandra, 47, suburban mom") and I need to refocus on *the change the customer is buying*. Especially powerful for positioning, brand voice, and "before/after" creative.

## Anti-pattern
Inventing aspirational jobs the customer would never say out loud. Klement's progress statement must be *grounded in actual interview language*, not the marketer's projected hero narrative.

## Source
Klement, *When Coffee and Kale Compete* (2016); jtbd.info "Replacing the User Story with the Job Story."
