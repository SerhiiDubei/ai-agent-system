# Bob Fitzpatrick's "The Mom Test"

## Definition
A protocol for customer interviews that *prevents* false validation. **Three rules**:
1. Talk about *their life*, not your idea
2. Ask about *specifics in the past*, not generics in the future
3. Talk *less, listen more*

The name: even your mom shouldn't be able to lie to you if you ask the questions right.

## Concrete protocol moves

| Bad question | Mom-Test question |
|---|---|
| "Would you use a product that…?" | "Tell me about the last time you had to deal with X." |
| "How important is X to you?" | "What have you done about X in the last month?" |
| "What features would you want?" | "Walk me through how you handled it last time. What was the worst part?" |

Listen for **commitment & advancement signals**: time, money, reputation. A "this is great" with no follow-up = compliment, not data.

## Cross-industry application

- **B2B SaaS pre-launch**: "Walk me through your last QBR prep — show me the doc you built." (You see the actual workflow, not the imagined one.)
- **Senior care**: don't ask "would you hire help?" — ask "tell me about the last hard week with your mom. What did you try?"
- **DTC supplement**: don't ask "would you take a daily greens powder?" — ask "what do you currently do in the morning for energy? When did you start?"
- **Fintech**: don't ask "do you struggle with debt?" — ask "show me your last 3 statements. Which bill do you open first?"

## When I reach for it
**Always** before drafting personas, especially when source material is thin and I'm tempted to invent. Also: when stakeholders are excited and validation feels easy — that's the highest-risk moment.

For LLM execution: when reconstructing plausible quotes for personas, always frame them as **past behavior** ("I slipped in the tub last February"), not future opinion ("I would value safety"). Past behavior = credible quote; future opinion = invented.

## Anti-pattern
Pitching the idea anywhere in the call/persona. Once you've pitched, the data is poisoned — the interviewee is now in *be polite* mode. For personas: never write a persona that is "saying nice things about the product." That's a testimonial, not a persona.
