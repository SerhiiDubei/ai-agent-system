# Health Belief Model

## Definition
Originally a public-health framework (Rosenstock, 1974) explaining why people do/don't adopt preventive behaviors. Six constructs:

1. **Perceived susceptibility** — am I at risk?
2. **Perceived severity** — how bad?
3. **Perceived benefits** — will the action help?
4. **Perceived barriers** — cost, hassle, embarrassment
5. **Cues to action** — a trigger
6. **Self-efficacy** — can I actually do this?

Each construct maps to a copy decision.

## Application examples (commercial, not just public health)

- **Term life insurance**: susceptibility = "1 in 4 men your age have a cardiac event before 55"; severity = family financial ruin; benefit = lump sum; barrier = "medical exam, takes hours"; cue = a friend's diagnosis; self-efficacy = "60-second quote."
- **Mattress**: susceptibility = "your back pain compounds"; severity = chronic disability; barrier = "what if I hate it"; cue = waking up sore again; self-efficacy = 100-night trial.
- **Senior fall-detection wearable**: susceptibility = 1-in-4 over 65 fall annually; severity = fractured hip → 50% don't recover; barrier = "Mom won't wear it"; cue = a near-miss; self-efficacy = "set up in 5 minutes by anyone."
- **B2B cybersecurity SaaS**: susceptibility = breach probability; severity = regulatory fines; benefit = compliance; barrier = procurement; cue = peer-company breach in the news.
- **DTC women's health (PCOS supplement)**: susceptibility = "1 in 10 women have it undiagnosed"; severity = fertility, weight, mood; barrier = "I've tried things"; cue = doctor dismissed her; self-efficacy = simple regimen.

## When I reach for it
**Anything health-adjacent, fear-driven, or preventive.** Also: anything where the *barrier* (not the benefit) is what's actually killing conversion. For senior care, telehealth, insurance — ALWAYS USE.

For each persona in a health-adjacent brief, my pain_points should include explicit barrier language and my decision_triggers should include explicit self-efficacy reassurance ("60-second quote", "set up in 5 minutes").

## Anti-pattern
Cranking susceptibility/severity (fear) without paired self-efficacy. Fear without "you can do this" produces avoidance, not purchase. The model is symmetric on purpose.

## Source
Rosenstock, "Historical Origins of the Health Belief Model" (Health Education Monographs, 1974); Champion & Skinner in *Health Behavior and Health Education* (Glanz et al., 4th ed., 2008); CDC Health Communication Playbook.
