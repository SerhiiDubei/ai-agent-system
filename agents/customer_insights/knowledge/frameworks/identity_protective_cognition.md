# Identity-Protective Cognition (Dan Kahan)

## Definition
People process information in ways that **protect their identity within their cultural in-group**. When facts threaten group identity, people don't update — they reason *harder against* the facts. Kahan's work shows numeracy and education *amplify* identity-protective bias rather than dissolving it.

For commerce: **people don't buy products that contradict who they believe themselves to be**, regardless of evidence the product works.

## Application examples

- **DTC supplements/wellness**: the "natural/clean" identity rejects pharma-coded language even for the same molecule. Selling NAC as "pharmaceutical-grade" loses; "from clean fermentation" wins for that tribe.
- **Fintech**: "self-made" identity refuses anything labeled "subprime" or "bad credit," even when it's exactly their product. Reframe as "rebuilders" or "next chapter."
- **Senior care**: elders' identity = "independent." Any product framed as "for people who can't manage" gets refused. Reframe as "for people who plan ahead."
- **B2B SaaS**: "we're a Google Sheets shop, not an enterprise" identity blocks adoption of software that is objectively better. Reframe as "spreadsheet-native" or "what your sheet should grow into."
- **Healthcare (mental health)**: "I'm not the kind of person who needs therapy" — Cerebral, BetterHelp got around this with "coaching"/"talk to someone" framing.

## When I reach for it
**When the audience SHOULD obviously buy and doesn't.** The block is almost always identity, not feature. Also: any niche touching aging, decline, financial difficulty, or health vulnerability — these are inherently identity-sensitive.

For each persona in identity-sensitive briefs, my pain_points and trust_needs should include identity-preservation language ("for people who plan ahead", "for the next chapter", "for people who handle their own decisions"), and my objections should include identity-rejection scripts ("I'm not the kind of person who...").

## Anti-pattern
Trying to argue people out of identity with more proof. More evidence makes it worse. The play is **re-categorization**: change which tribe the product belongs to, not the customer.

## Source
Kahan, "Ideology, Motivated Reasoning, and Cognitive Reflection" (Judgment and Decision Making, 2013); Kahan et al., "Motivated Numeracy and Enlightened Self-Government" (Behavioural Public Policy, 2017); Cultural Cognition Project, Yale.
