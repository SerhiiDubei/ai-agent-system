# Maslow's Hierarchy → Pain Points

## Definition
Maslow proposed five tiers (1943): physiological → safety → belonging → esteem → self-actualization. Academically contested as a literal hierarchy, but operationally useful as a **pain-and-promise ladder**: every product simultaneously sells on multiple tiers, and the strongest creative explicitly addresses the *lowest unmet tier* the customer is conscious of.

## Marketer's working model

| Tier | Pain language | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Physiological** | pain, sleep, energy, hunger | "I haven't slept through the night in 3 years." |
| **Safety** | financial security, health predictability, family safety | "Will my kids be ok if something happens?" |
| **Belonging** | loneliness, community, romantic acceptance | "I'm the only one in my friend group still single." |
| **Esteem** | competence, status, recognition | "I want my team to respect my decisions." |
| **Self-actualization** | meaning, growth, legacy | "I want to leave something behind." |

## Cross-industry application

- **Senior care**: sells on safety (Mom's safety) + esteem (I'm a good daughter) + belonging (so Dad isn't lonely)
- **DTC supplements**: physiological (energy) → esteem (look good) → self-actualization (best version)
- **Fintech (insurance)**: pure safety + belonging (protect family)
- **B2B SaaS (dev tool)**: esteem (status with peers) + self-actualization (craft)
- **Telehealth**: physiological (sleep, panic) + safety (not losing my job)

## When I reach for it
**Whenever I need to derive the deeper-tier emotional pain underneath a surface-level functional pain.** "Fear of falling" → safety → belonging (independence in own home) → esteem (not being a burden) → self-actualization (aging on my own terms). The deepest layer is usually the real driver and lives in the persona's primary_job emotional component.

## Anti-pattern
Picking one tier and assuming it's "the" motivation. Real buyers move between tiers in a single session — the body says "I'm tired" but the click happens because of "I deserve this."

## Source
Maslow, "A Theory of Human Motivation" (1943); Drew Eric Whitman *Cashvertising* (LF8 framework — direct descendant); Saul McLeod, "Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs" (Simply Psychology, peer-reviewed update).
