# Bob Moesta's Forces of Progress

## Definition
A purchase decision is a tug-of-war between four forces. **Push** of the situation = pain with status quo. **Pull** of the new solution = attraction. **Anxiety** of the new = uncertainty/risk of switching. **Habit** of the present = inertia and sunk-cost loyalty. A switch happens only when **Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit**.

The "Switch Interview" reconstructs the timeline from "first thought" to "first use," anchoring on observable events, not opinions.

## Interview protocol (6 steps)

1. Identify the moment of *first thought* ("when did you first realize you might need something different?").
2. Walk forward chronologically — **events, not feelings**: who was in the room, what was said, what was browsed.
3. Identify the *trigger event* (often emotional: a fight, a fall, a missed deadline, a doctor's word).
4. Map the *consideration set* (including non-obvious competitors: "doing nothing," "asking sister").
5. Identify *anxieties* — what almost stopped them — and *habits* they had to override.
6. Pinpoint the *moment of commitment* (often pre-purchase: "the moment you knew you'd buy").

## Cross-industry application examples

- **Mattress (DTC)**: Push = back pain. Pull = "bed in a box, 100-night trial." Anxiety = "what if it sags." Habit = "we've had this Sealy 12 years."
- **B2B SaaS switch from spreadsheets**: Push = VLOOKUP broke during board prep. Pull = real-time dashboards. Anxiety = data migration, IT review. Habit = "Sheila knows the spreadsheet."
- **Senior care**: Push = Mom left stove on. Pull = aide 4hrs/day. Anxiety = "she'll feel I don't trust her." Habit = "we've always handled it ourselves."
- **Debt relief**: Push = collections call at work. Pull = one monthly payment. Anxiety = "is this a scam, will it tank my credit." Habit = "I've always just paid the minimums."

## When I reach for it
**When source material is thin** — I mentally simulate this 4-force timeline to find the trigger event. Also when writing copy / objection handling — Forces map directly to message hierarchy (lead with Push, prove the Pull, dissolve the Anxiety, name the Habit). Use for B2B/multi-stakeholder briefs especially.

## Anti-pattern
Treating Forces as a survey ("rate your anxiety 1-7"). It's a *reconstruction of a specific event*, not a Likert scale. Also: skipping Habit. Most "we lost the deal" post-mortems blame price; the real winner was usually inertia.
