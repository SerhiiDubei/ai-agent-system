# Caregiver Burden Framework (Zarit + Pearlin)

## Definition (NICHE-SPECIFIC: medical, senior care, chronic illness)

The **Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI)** quantifies subjective caregiver strain across 22 items in five dimensions: personal strain, role strain, financial strain, **guilt**, and **loss of self**.

**Pearlin's Caregiver Stress Process Model** distinguishes:
- **Primary stressors** (objective care tasks, cognitive decline of patient)
- **Secondary stressors** (role conflict, financial spillover)
- **Mediators** (social support, coping style)

Together they explain why two caregivers with identical tasks have wildly different breaking points.

## Application examples

- **Senior care marketing**: leads aren't the elder — they're the burned-out caregiver. Copy that names *guilt* + *loss of self* outconverts copy about elder benefits 2-3× in tested campaigns.
- **Chronic illness DTC** (e.g. diabetes supplies): spouse-as-caregiver is often the buyer; messaging to "the person managing your loved one's diabetes" surfaces a hidden segment.
- **Mental health (telehealth)**: the *parent of an adolescent in crisis* is a Pearlin-style overloaded caregiver; positioning around *"you don't have to hold this alone"* lands.
- **Hospice / palliative**: burden language ("you've been carrying this") is the entry point; product features come later.

## When I reach for it
**Only** when there is an unpaid third-party doing care labor between the patient and the purchase. If buyer = patient, this framework adds noise.

For walk-in tubs, mobility aids, hearing aids, Medicare advisory, senior living, hospice → ALMOST ALWAYS APPLIES. The decision_helper persona's pain_points should reference burden dimensions: guilt, role conflict, loss of self, financial strain.

## Anti-pattern
Using burden language for elders themselves — they often resent being framed as a burden. The framework describes the *caregiver's* experience and should be aimed at the caregiver as audience. Mismatched audience = offended elder.

## Source
Zarit, Reever & Bach-Peterson, "Relatives of the impaired elderly" (Gerontologist, 1980); Pearlin et al., "Caregiving and the Stress Process" (Gerontologist, 1990); AARP *Caregiving in the U.S.* reports (current).
